How Singapore withholding tax works

The four rules that decide your rate

Where the work is done

Service fees are only subject to Singapore withholding tax if the work is performed in Singapore. Remote work done entirely overseas is outside the net.

Domestic rates

Interest 15%, royalties 10%, equipment rent 15%, services at the corporate rate (17%), non-resident directors 24%, non-resident professionals 15% of gross.

Treaty relief needs proof

Singapore has tax treaties with around 100 jurisdictions. To apply a lower treaty rate, keep a Certificate of Residence from the payee's tax authority on file.

File by the 15th

File Form IR37 and pay by the 15th of the second month after payment. Late payment costs 5%, plus 1% a month up to another 15%.

Frequently asked

Withholding tax questions

Only if the services are performed in Singapore. Fees for work done wholly outside Singapore, for example a consultant or agency working remotely from overseas, are not subject to Singapore withholding tax. For work done in Singapore, the rate is the prevailing corporate tax rate (17%), unless a tax treaty exempts it because the foreign company has no permanent establishment in Singapore.

The domestic rates are 15% on interest and 10% on royalties paid to non-residents. A tax treaty can reduce them, for example interest to a UAE company is 0% and royalties to a Malaysian company are 8%. The payee must give a Certificate of Residence from its home tax authority to claim the treaty rate.

The payer must file Form IR37 and pay the tax by the 15th of the second month after the date of payment. A payment made on 10 November 2026 is due by 15 January 2027.

IRAS imposes a 5% penalty on unpaid tax after the due date, and a further 1% for each completed month it stays unpaid, up to a maximum of 15% more.

The Singapore payer. If the agreement is net of tax, the payer bears the tax and it is grossed up: gross amount = net amount divided by (1 minus the rate). For a 10% royalty on a S$9,000 net payment, the gross is S$10,000 and the tax S$1,000.

No comprehensive treaty. Singapore's agreements with the US and Hong Kong cover only shipping and air transport income, so the domestic withholding rates apply to interest, royalties and services.
