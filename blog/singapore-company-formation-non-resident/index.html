Singapore Company Formation for Non-Residents (2026): How to Set Up and Run One Without Moving

📅 Published 24 Nov 2026 ⏱ 9 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

Non-residents can own 100% of a Singapore private limited company and incorporate it entirely online, without visiting. Three things have to be arranged locally: a director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore (usually a nominee director, unless you relocate on an Employment Pass), an ACRA-registered corporate service provider to file the incorporation (foreigners without Singpass cannot file themselves), and a Singapore registered office address. The government fees are S$315. The parts that need most planning are opening a bank account and making sure the company is actually tax resident in Singapore, because a company managed entirely from abroad may not get Singapore tax treaty benefits.

  • 100% foreign ownership is allowed. There is no local shareholding requirement to incorporate.
  • You need one locally resident director: a nominee director, a trusted local, or yourself on an Employment Pass.
  • Foreigners without Singpass must file through an ACRA-registered corporate service provider.
  • Bank account opening, not incorporation, is usually the slowest step for non-residents.
  • Tax residency depends on where the company is controlled and managed. Without it, you lose treaty benefits and the start-up tax exemption.
  • Running the company from abroad needs no Singapore visa. Working in Singapore does.

"Singapore company formation for non-residents" is one of the most searched incorporation queries in the world, from founders in India, the US, the UK, Australia, the UAE and Southeast Asia. The short answer is yes: you can form and own a Singapore company without living there. The longer answer is that three rules shape how you do it, and two practical issues, banking and tax residency, decide whether the structure actually works. This guide covers all of them.

What non-residents can and cannot do

Rule 1: The locally resident director

Every Singapore company needs at least one director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore. That means a Singapore Citizen, a PR, or a holder of an Employment Pass, Personalised Employment Pass or ONE Pass. Non-residents have four ways to meet the rule:

OptionHow it worksBest for
Nominee directorA CSP provides a qualified resident to sit on the board. The nominee does not run the business, and you keep control through the shareholding and an agreement.Founders who will stay abroad
Relocate on an Employment PassThe company sponsors your EP and you become the resident director. A nominee often covers the gap until the EP is approved.Founders moving to Singapore
Local co-founder or hireA Singaporean, PR or pass holder you trust joins the board.Teams with a Singapore presence
ONE Pass / PEPIf you already hold one, you qualify yourself.High earners and established talent

Nominee directors carry real legal duties, and since 2025 Singapore has tightened the rules: nominees must be arranged through registered CSPs, and nominee status is disclosed to ACRA. Read what the nominee director changes mean and our complete nominee director guide. Published market rates for a nominee director are commonly around S$2,000 a year, often with a refundable deposit.

Rule 2: File through a registered corporate service provider

Unless you have Singpass, you cannot log in to BizFile yourself. ACRA's guidance is that foreigners must engage a corporate service provider to reserve the name and register the company. Choose one that is registered with ACRA as a CSP; you can verify this free on BizFile. A good CSP will also:

  • run your KYC (identity, address and source of funds checks);
  • prepare the constitution, first board resolutions and share certificates;
  • provide the registered office address and company secretary;
  • arrange the nominee director if you need one.

Rule 3: A Singapore registered office

The company needs a physical registered office address in Singapore. Non-residents normally use their corporate secretary's address. Mail is received and scanned, and statutory records are kept there. See the registered office guide.

The remote formation process, step by step

  1. Choose a name and check it with our Company Name Checker.
  2. Pick your SSIC code with the SSIC Code Search. Banks look closely at this.
  3. Complete KYC with your CSP. Usually a passport copy, proof of residential address dated within the last few months, and a short description of the business and its source of funds. Some CSPs require certified or apostilled copies. See our apostille guide.
  4. Arrange the resident director and sign the nominee agreement, if any.
  5. CSP reserves the name (S$15) and files the incorporation (S$300) on BizFile.
  6. Receive your UEN and Business Profile, often within 1 to 3 working days of filing.
  7. Open the bank account (see below).
  8. Set up compliance: secretary, registers, financial year end and tax calendar.

Full detail on each step is in how to register a company on BizFile, and the full list of requirements is in our requirements checklist.

Opening a bank account as a non-resident

This is where most non-resident formations slow down. Incorporation can take days; a bank account can take weeks. What helps:

  • A clear business story: who your customers are, where money will come from and go to, and why Singapore. Vague "general trading" profiles get the most questions.
  • Consistent documents: the Business Profile, constitution, a board resolution to open the account, and KYC for every director and shareholder above the bank's ownership threshold.
  • Be ready for a meeting. Some banks require directors to attend in person or by video.
  • Consider a digital business account to start trading while a traditional bank account is being processed.

The tax trap: is your company really Singapore tax resident?

A Singapore company is tax resident in Singapore if it is controlled and managed in Singapore, which in practice means that strategic decisions are made and board meetings held there. A company whose directors all sit and decide abroad can be incorporated in Singapore yet not be Singapore tax resident. That matters because:

  • only tax-resident companies can get a Certificate of Residence to claim treaty benefits, such as lower withholding tax on payments from India or other treaty countries;
  • the start-up tax exemption is only available to tax-resident companies;
  • your home country may treat a company managed from there as its own tax resident. Examples are India's place-of-effective-management rules and the US CFC rules covered in our GILTI guide.

IRAS is explicit about this. It grants a Certificate of Residence only for a calendar year in which control and management were exercised in Singapore. It generally does not issue one to foreign-owned investment holding companies or nominee companies unless they can show real management in Singapore, such as at least one Singapore-based director in an executive role who is not a nominee director. A nominee director lets you incorporate, but it does not by itself make the company tax resident.

If treaty benefits matter, plan for genuine Singapore management: board meetings held in Singapore, a resident director with real involvement, or relocating yourself. Model the numbers with the Singapore vs Home Tax tool.

Running the company from abroad

  • Annual compliance can be done remotely: annual return, ECI, Form C-S and GST filings are all online, and your secretary and accountant handle the filings.
  • Working in Singapore needs a work pass. If you plan to spend real working time there, look at the Employment Pass for company directors.
  • Home-country reporting: Indian residents must follow ODI/FEMA rules (see FEMA questions answered), and US persons have Form 5471 and FBAR obligations.
