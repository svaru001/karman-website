How to Register a Sole Proprietorship in Singapore (2026): Fees, Foreigner Rules, and When an LLP or Pte Ltd Is Better

📅 Published 24 Nov 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

You register a sole proprietorship in Singapore on ACRA BizFile: reserve a business name (S$15), then register the business for S$100 for one year, or S$160 for three years if CPF conditions are met. You must be at least 18. Locals and foreigners with Singpass can file themselves; foreigners without Singpass must use a corporate service provider, and if the owner lives overseas the business needs an authorised representative who is ordinarily resident in Singapore. A sole proprietorship is not a separate legal entity: profits are taxed at your personal income tax rates and you are personally liable for its debts.

  • Fees: S$15 name application + S$100 (1-year registration) or S$160 (3-year, subject to CPF conditions).
  • Owner must be 18 or above. Locals and foreigners with Singpass can file on BizFile directly.
  • Foreigners without Singpass must use a CSP. Owners living overseas need a locally resident authorised representative.
  • No separate legal entity: unlimited personal liability, taxed at personal rates up to 24%.
  • Self-employed people earning more than S$6,000 a year in net trade income must contribute to MediSave.
  • An LLP needs at least 2 partners and a local manager; registration is S$100 plus the name fee.

A sole proprietorship is the simplest and cheapest way to start a business in Singapore. It suits freelancers, consultants, tutors, home-based sellers and anyone testing an idea before committing to a company. It also comes with trade-offs that many people only discover later. This guide covers how to register one with ACRA, what it costs, the rules for foreigners, and when a limited liability partnership (LLP) or private limited company makes more sense.

What a sole proprietorship is

  • A business owned by one person, registered with ACRA under a business name.
  • It is not a separate legal entity. You and the business are legally the same, so you are personally liable for all its debts.
  • Profits are added to your personal income and taxed at personal rates.
  • Two or more owners with the same structure form a partnership, registered the same way.

Who can register

  • You must be at least 18 years old and not an undischarged bankrupt.
  • Singapore Citizens, PRs and foreigners with Singpass can register directly on BizFile.
  • Foreigners without Singpass must have a corporate service provider (CSP) register the business for them.
  • If the owner lives overseas, the business must appoint an authorised representative who is ordinarily resident in Singapore.

On a work pass?

Registering a business is not the same as being allowed to work in it. If you are in Singapore on an Employment Pass, S Pass or Dependant's Pass, check MOM's rules before running a side business. A Pte Ltd with a proper work pass is usually the cleaner route for foreigners who want to operate in Singapore. See can a foreigner start a business in Singapore.

Fees

Registration for 1 yearS$100
Registration for 3 yearsS$160, available only if CPF Board conditions are met
Total to start (1 year)S$115

Compare that with S$315 for a Pte Ltd, and remember that a company also needs a company secretary. See our cost guide.

How to register, step by step

  1. Choose a business name. It cannot be identical to an existing name, and sensitive words are referred to other agencies. Check it with our Name Checker.
  2. Reserve the name on BizFile (S$15) with Singpass, choosing your SSIC code (use the SSIC Code Search). Only the person who reserved the name can complete the registration.
  3. Register the business within the reservation period: business address (not a PO box), your residential and contact addresses, and the registration term (1 or 3 years). Pay the fee.
  4. Receive your UEN and Business Profile. See how to use your UEN.
  5. Get licences if your activity needs them, for example food, education or employment agency licences.
  6. Renew before expiry every 1 or 3 years. An expired registration means you are trading unregistered.

Tax and CPF

  • Income tax: business profits are declared in your personal tax return (Form B) and taxed at progressive personal rates, up to 24% at the top band. A Pte Ltd pays a flat 17% with exemptions that cut the effective rate on the first profits. Compare with the Singapore Tax Calculator.
  • MediSave: if your annual net trade income (gross trade income minus allowable expenses, capital allowances and trade losses) is above S$6,000, CPF Board requires you to contribute to MediSave as a self-employed person.
  • GST: register if taxable turnover exceeds S$1 million. See when to register for GST.

What about an LLP?

A limited liability partnership sits between a partnership and a company. Partners get limited liability for the LLP's debts, but profits are still taxed in each partner's hands.

LLP requirementRule
PartnersAt least 2, individuals or companies
Local managerAt least 1 manager who meets the local residency rules
FeesS$15 name application + S$100 registration
Registered officeA Singapore address
EndorsementOther position holders must endorse the registration within 60 days, unless a CSP filed it

LLPs are popular with professional firms (law, accounting, consulting) that want limited liability without corporate tax.

When to switch to a Pte Ltd

  • Profits are high enough that the 17% corporate rate and start-up exemption beat your personal rate.
  • You want to limit personal liability, for example when signing large contracts or leases or hiring staff.
  • You plan to raise money or bring in partners.
  • Clients or platforms insist on dealing with a company.

You cannot convert a sole proprietorship directly into a company. You incorporate a new Pte Ltd, transfer the business into it, then terminate the sole proprietorship. Read the full structure comparison, try the Business Structure Recommender, or see how to register a Pte Ltd.
