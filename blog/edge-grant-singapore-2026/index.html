EDGE Grant Singapore (2026): What Replaced EDG, PSG and MRA, Who Qualifies and How to Apply

📅 Published 23 Nov 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

Enterprise Singapore closed the Enterprise Development Grant (EDG), Productivity Solutions Grant (PSG) and Market Readiness Assistance (MRA) on 29 September 2026 and replaced them with one scheme, the EDGE Grant, from 30 September 2026. EDGE covers over 150 activities across 8 business areas, funds up to 70% of qualifying costs for SMEs and up to 50% for non-SMEs depending on the activity, and is capped at S$100,000 per company per year. Applicants must be registered in Singapore with at least 30% local (Singaporean or PR) shareholding, and must apply on the Business Grants Portal before starting work or paying any vendor.

  • EDG, PSG and MRA stopped taking applications on 29 September 2026. Applications and projects already in progress continue to be processed.
  • EDGE is not an acronym. It is one grant covering 8 business areas and over 150 activities.
  • Support: up to 70% for SMEs and up to 50% for non-SMEs, but the level differs by activity. Check the specific activity page.
  • Cap: S$100,000 per company per year, refreshed every 1 April, with a S$30,000 sub-cap for digital solutions, enterprise systems and pre-scoped equipment.
  • You need at least 30% Singaporean or PR shareholding. A 100% foreign-owned company does not qualify.
  • Never start work or pay a deposit before you submit. Retrospective applications are rejected.

If you were planning a PSG-funded accounting system or an MRA-funded overseas trade fair, the scheme you were reading about no longer exists. On 30 September 2026 Enterprise Singapore folded its three main SME grants into a single programme called the EDGE Grant. Many vendor pages and older guides still describe the old schemes. This guide sets out what changed, based on Enterprise Singapore's own EDGE pages and FAQ.

What changed on 30 September 2026

Old schemeWhat it fundedStatus
Enterprise Development Grant (EDG)Larger upgrading, innovation and overseas expansion projectsCeased 29 Sep 2026
Productivity Solutions Grant (PSG)Pre-approved IT solutions and equipmentCeased 29 Sep 2026
Market Readiness Assistance (MRA)Overseas market promotion, business development and set-upCeased 29 Sep 2026
EDGE GrantAll of the above, under one frameworkOpen from 30 Sep 2026

Already applied under the old schemes? Enterprise Singapore says ongoing EDG, MRA and PSG applications and projects continue to be processed, and you submit your claim when the project is completed. You do not need to reapply under EDGE.

The 8 business areas

EDGE groups more than 150 supportable activities into 8 business areas. Enterprise Singapore's EDGE page names these:

  • Automation and Digitalisation: the old PSG territory, including digital solutions, enterprise systems and equipment.
  • Business Strategy
  • Financial Management
  • Innovation
  • Internationalisation: the old MRA territory, covering overseas market entry and promotion.
  • Standards
  • Sustainability

Each activity has its own page on Enterprise Singapore or GoBusiness with the exact scope, eligible costs and support level. Treat those pages as the source of truth for your project, not a vendor's brochure.

How much EDGE pays

ItemRule
Support levelUp to 70% of qualifying costs for SMEs and up to 50% for non-SMEs. Enterprise Singapore's FAQ notes that many activities are funded at up to 50% (SMEs) and 30% (non-SMEs), with the higher 70% / 50% tier for internationalisation and sustainability activities. The level differs by activity.
Annual capS$100,000 per company per year across all EDGE activities, refreshed every 1 April (the grant year runs 1 April to 31 March).
Digital sub-capWithin the S$100,000, up to S$30,000 for single-function digital solutions, integrated enterprise systems and pre-scoped equipment.
How it is paidReimbursement. You complete the project, pay the vendor in full, then submit a claim.

The S$100,000 annual cap is the change that catches companies out. Under the old schemes a large EDG project and an MRA trip were separate budgets. Under EDGE they share one yearly pool, so plan which projects matter most before you apply for the first one.

Who qualifies

  • Registered in Singapore. Non-profits, societies and similar entities are not eligible.
  • At least 30% local shareholding, held by Singapore Citizens and/or Permanent Residents. Other requirements can apply depending on the activity.
  • SME status decides your support level. Enterprise Singapore defines an SME as a company with group annual sales turnover of not more than S$100 million or group employment of not more than 200 workers. "Group" includes any company that owns more than 50% of you and any company you own more than 50% of.

Foreign founders: check the 30% rule first

A Singapore Pte Ltd that is 100% owned by foreign shareholders, which is the usual structure for founders from India, the UAE, the US or the UK, does not meet EDGE's 30% local shareholding requirement. Bringing in a Singaporean or PR co-founder or investor changes that, but it should be a business decision first, not a grant decision. Foreign-owned companies should look at tax incentives such as the start-up tax exemption, which has no local shareholding rule.

Digital solutions and equipment (the old PSG route)

Pre-approved solutions did not disappear. They now sit inside EDGE's Automation and Digitalisation area, under the S$30,000 sub-cap:

  • Single-function digital solutions (for example accounting, HR or inventory software) are listed on IMDA's SMEs Go Digital site, which has a package builder.
  • Integrated enterprise systems (ERP) must cover at least 3 core functions. A pre-approved vendor package takes about 1 week to process; using your own vendor, where no suitable package exists, takes about 10 weeks.
  • You can make one application per UEN for single-function digital solutions and integrated systems. Pre-scoped equipment can be claimed for more than one location (by postal code).
  • Equipment bought with the grant carries a 1-year holding period from claim approval. Do not sell or move it out during that time.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Check eligibility: 30% local shareholding and SME status, using your latest group revenue and headcount.
  2. Find the activity page for your project on Enterprise Singapore or GoBusiness and confirm the support level and eligible costs.
  3. Get a quotation from your vendor or consultant. Do not sign, start work or pay a deposit yet.
  4. Set up Corppass: the person submitting needs the Business Grants Portal (BGP) Preparer or Acceptor role.
  5. Apply on the Business Grants Portal through the link on the activity page.
  6. Run the project after you submit. Paying after submission but before approval is allowed, but you bear the risk if the application is rejected.
  7. Claim on BGP after completion and full payment, with invoices and proof of payment.

Mistakes that get EDGE applications rejected

  • Retrospective applications. Enterprise Singapore treats an application as retrospective if you started work or paid any payment or deposit to a vendor before submitting. These are not supported.
  • Shareholding below 30% local, often after a fundraise or share transfer diluted the Singaporean founders.
  • Forgetting the shared S$100,000 cap and running out of headroom for a more important project later in the grant year.
  • Relying on old PSG or MRA material. Vendors and blogs still quote the old schemes. Always check the EDGE activity page.

Other grants still running

EDGE replaced three schemes, not every grant. The Business Adaptation Grant (BizAdapt) for companies affected by US tariffs runs separately, with its own S$100,000 cap. For the wider picture, see our guide to Singapore startup grants.

If you are still setting up, make sure the company's shareholding and SSIC code fit the grants you plan to use. Karman can structure this at incorporation, so you don't have to restructure later.
