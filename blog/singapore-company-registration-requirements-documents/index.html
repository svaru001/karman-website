Singapore Company Registration Requirements and Documents (2026): The Complete Checklist

📅 Published 24 Nov 2026 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

To register a private limited company (Pte Ltd) in Singapore you need: an approved company name, at least one director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore (a Singapore Citizen, PR or Employment Pass, Personalised Employment Pass or ONE Pass holder), at least one shareholder (up to 50, any nationality, individuals or companies), a minimum share capital of S$1, a Singapore registered office address, a constitution, and a company secretary appointed within six months. ACRA charges S$15 for the name application and S$300 to incorporate. Foreigners without Singpass must file through an ACRA-registered corporate service provider.

  • At least 1 locally resident director: Singapore Citizen, PR, or EP, PEP or ONE Pass holder, aged 18 or above.
  • At least 1 shareholder, up to 50. Shareholders can be foreign individuals or companies, and can own 100%.
  • Minimum share capital of S$1. There is no minimum deposit to put in a bank before registering.
  • A Singapore registered office address, a constitution (the ACRA model is fine), and a company secretary within 6 months.
  • Government fees: S$15 name application + S$300 incorporation = S$315.
  • Foreigners without Singpass must file through an ACRA-registered corporate service provider (CSP).

"What do I need to register a company in Singapore?" is the first question almost every founder asks, and the answer is shorter than most people expect. Singapore has no minimum capital worth worrying about and no local shareholding requirement. You also don't need to visit to incorporate. What it does insist on is a locally resident director, a local address and a qualified company secretary. This checklist covers every requirement and every document for a private limited company (Pte Ltd), the structure used by almost all startups and SMEs. It is based on ACRA's own registration guidance.

1. Company name

Your proposed name must be approved by ACRA before you can incorporate. The name application costs S$15, which is not refunded if the name is rejected or you withdraw. Once approved, the name is reserved for 120 days, and you must incorporate within that window or the name is released.

ACRA rejects names that are identical to an existing entity's or a reserved name, or that are undesirable. Names with sensitive words, such as "bank", "finance", "law" or "university", are referred to other agencies. ACRA aims to review names within 3 working days, but a referral can take up to 15 working days.

Run your idea through our free Company Name Checker first. It flags common rejection reasons before you pay.

2. Directors

Every Singapore company needs at least one director who is ordinarily resident in Singapore. ACRA treats these people as meeting the local residency rules:

  • Singapore Citizens
  • Singapore Permanent Residents
  • holders of a valid Employment Pass, Personalised Employment Pass or Overseas Networks & Expertise (ONE) Pass

Every director must also be at least 18 years old, of full legal capacity, and not an undischarged bankrupt or disqualified from acting as a director. There is no maximum number of directors, and the other directors can live anywhere.

If you are a foreign founder

You have three options for the local director requirement:

  • Relocate: apply for an Employment Pass through your new company and become the resident director yourself.
  • Appoint someone you trust who already qualifies, such as a co-founder or senior hire.
  • Use a nominee director arranged through a registered CSP. Since 2025 nominee directors are subject to stricter disclosure rules. See what changed.

3. Shareholders

  • A private company can have 1 to 50 shareholders.
  • Shareholders can be individuals or companies, of any nationality. A foreign parent company or foreign individuals can own 100% of a Singapore Pte Ltd.
  • The same person can be both the sole shareholder and a director.
  • Shares can be ordinary or preference shares. Most new companies start with one class of ordinary shares.

Some grants require local shareholding. Enterprise Singapore's EDGE Grant, for example, requires at least 30% Singaporean or PR ownership. That is a reason to plan your cap table early, not a requirement to incorporate.

4. Share capital

The minimum share capital is S$1. You do not need to deposit capital in a bank before incorporating, and the figure can be in SGD or another currency. You can increase it later by allotting new shares. Read our guide to paid-up capital before choosing a number. Banks, landlords and visa applications sometimes look for more than S$1, and an Employment Pass application for a founder is stronger with a credible capital base.

5. Company secretary

  • Must be appointed within 6 months of incorporation. Leaving the role vacant longer can expose directors to a fine of up to S$1,000.
  • Must be a natural person who is locally resident (Singapore Citizen, PR or a qualifying pass holder).
  • Cannot be the same person as the sole director. A one-person company therefore needs a separate secretary.

Most founders outsource this role. A good corporate secretary also keeps your statutory registers and tracks AGM and annual return deadlines.

6. Registered office address

The company must have a physical registered office address in Singapore. A PO box doesn't qualify. It is where official correspondence goes and where company records can be kept. It does not have to be where you operate. Many foreign-owned and home-based companies use a corporate secretary's address. Rules on registered office accessibility changed in October 2026. See our summary of the ACRA changes and the registered office guide.

7. Constitution

The constitution sets out how the company is run: share transfers, director powers, meetings. You can upload a custom constitution or adopt ACRA's model constitution during registration. The model works for most single-founder and small companies. Co-founder and investor-backed companies often add pre-emption rights, drag-along and tag-along clauses, and share transfer restrictions. Our constitution guide explains when to customise.

8. Auditor

A company must appoint an auditor within 3 months of incorporation unless it is exempt. Most new companies qualify as a small company, which is exempt from audit. The usual test is meeting at least two of these three criteria: annual revenue of S$10 million or less, total assets of S$10 million or less, and 50 or fewer employees. Group rules apply if the company has a parent or subsidiaries. Check ACRA's audit exemption guidance for your situation.

9. Business activity (SSIC code)

You must declare up to two business activities using SSIC codes. The codes affect which agencies review your application, which licences you need, and how banks see your risk profile. Find yours with the SSIC Code Search.

Document checklist

ACRA does not ask for a stack of uploads for a standard Pte Ltd. Most information is typed into BizFile. Gather this before you start:

ForWhat you need
The companyApproved name; financial year end; registered office address; company email; SSIC code(s); share capital and share structure; constitution (custom or model)
Each director, shareholder and secretaryFull name as in ID; NRIC (Singaporeans and PRs) or FIN or passport number; date of birth; nationality; residential address; contact address in the same country as the residential address (not a PO box); mobile number and email
Corporate shareholdersName and registration number of the shareholding company, its registered address, and an authorised representative
RegistersDetails for the Register of Registrable Controllers (beneficial owners) and the Register of Nominee Directors and Shareholders, unless exempt

If a CSP files for you, it will also ask for KYC documents under anti-money-laundering rules: usually a passport copy, proof of residential address dated within the last few months, and sometimes a CV or source-of-funds explanation. Non-English documents need certified translations. Get a personalised list from our Document Checklist tool.

What you get after incorporation

  • A notification of successful incorporation with your UEN (Unique Entity Number). Learn how to look up and use your UEN.
  • A link to a free Business Profile. Download it within 60 days, as banks ask for it.
  • You can apply for Corppass one day after the UEN is issued. You need it for IRAS, CPF and most government e-services.

Requirements that come after incorporation

  • Open a corporate bank account. Use our bank account recommender.
  • Keep statutory registers from day one, including members, directors, controllers and nominees.
  • Appoint the secretary (6 months) and auditor (3 months) if not already done.
  • Register for GST if taxable turnover will exceed S$1 million. See when to register.
  • Diary the annual return, ECI and Form C-S deadlines. Our 90-day checklist lists every step.
