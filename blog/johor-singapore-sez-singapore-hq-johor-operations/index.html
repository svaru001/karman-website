Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (2026): How to Structure a Singapore HQ With Johor Operations

📅 Published 27 Nov 2026 ⏱ 11 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

The Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ), agreed in January 2025, covers over 3,500 km2 of southern Johor across nine flagship zones. Malaysia offers a 5% corporate tax rate for up to 15 years for new investments in qualifying activities such as AI, medical devices, aerospace manufacturing and global services hubs, and a 15% flat personal tax rate for eligible knowledge workers for 10 years. The incentives go to a Malaysian company operating in Johor, not to a Singapore company. The common structure is a Singapore holding and regional headquarters company that owns a Johor subsidiary for manufacturing, engineering or back-office work, with arm's length pricing between the two. The RTS Link between Johor Bahru and Woodlands is now targeted to open in February 2027.

  • JS-SEZ: agreed 7 January 2025, over 3,500 km2, nine flagship zones in southern Johor.
  • 5% Malaysian corporate tax for up to 15 years for new investments in qualifying activities, subject to MIDA approval.
  • 15% flat personal income tax for eligible knowledge workers for 10 years.
  • Incentives are granted to a Malaysian company in Johor. A Singapore entity alone does not qualify.
  • JS-SEZ incentives and other MIDA incentives (Pioneer Status, ITA) are mutually exclusive.
  • RTS Link Johor Bahru-Woodlands: passenger service now targeted for February 2027.

For decades, companies have run a quiet "twin hub" across the Causeway: management, finance and customers in Singapore, factories and larger teams in Johor. The Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ) turns that model into government policy on both sides, with Malaysian tax incentives, a one-stop investment centre and faster border crossings. With the RTS Link due to open in early 2027, 2026 is when many companies are deciding how to use it.

This guide explains what the JS-SEZ offers, who qualifies, and how to structure a Singapore company and a Johor company so that both work together.

What the JS-SEZ is

  • Agreed: exchanged by Malaysia and Singapore on 7 January 2025 at the 11th Leaders' Retreat. Malaysia announced its incentive package the next day.
  • Area: more than 3,500 km2, entirely within Johor. It is not a single industrial park.
  • Nine flagship zones (per MIDA): Johor Bahru City Centre, Iskandar Puteri, Tanjung Pelepas-Tanjung Bin, Pasir Gudang, Senai-Skudai, Sedenak, Forest City, Pengerang Integrated Petroleum Complex and Desaru.
  • Targets: 50 high-impact projects and 20,000 skilled jobs within five years, rising to 100 projects within a decade.

Three points are often missed:

  1. It is not automatic. Each incentive is approved by MIDA with conditions. Applicants must first get an official confirmation from IMFC-J (Invest Malaysia Facilitation Centre-Johor) that their location is within a flagship zone.
  2. It is not zone-wide. The qualifying activities differ by flagship zone, so the site you pick decides which incentives you can claim.
  3. It goes to a Malaysian company. A Singapore company does not receive Malaysian incentives. You need a Malaysian subsidiary (Sdn Bhd) carrying on the qualifying activity in Johor.

The twin-hub structure

Most companies using the JS-SEZ run two companies, each doing what it is best at:

Singapore Pte Ltd (HQ)Johor Sdn Bhd (operations)
Typical roleGroup holding, regional management, sales and contracts, treasury, IP ownership, fundraisingManufacturing, assembly, engineering, data centre operations, shared services, R&D support
Tax17% headline with exemptions; dividends to shareholders not taxed; generally no capital gains tax24% standard, or 5% under an approved JS-SEZ incentive
Why hereRule of law, banks, investors, customers, tax treaties, and a base for ASEANLand, larger workforce and lower operating costs, within an hour of Singapore
Key complianceLocal resident director, company secretary, annual return, Form CMalaysian director residence rules, SSM filings, MIDA incentive conditions

Profits move between the two through intercompany pricing: the Johor company typically charges the Singapore company for manufacturing or services, and the Singapore company earns the margin on sales, IP or management. Both IRAS and Malaysia's tax authority expect this pricing to be arm's length and documented. A 5% incentive in Johor does not let you shift profit there artificially, and a Singapore HQ with no real people cannot keep profit that belongs to Johor. Model scenarios with our transfer pricing margin simulator.

Large groups should also check the global minimum tax. Groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more are subject to Pillar Two rules, which can top up low-taxed profits to 15%. See Pillar Two and Singapore holding companies.

Who benefits most

  • Manufacturers in medical devices, aerospace, semiconductors and electronics that need floor space Singapore cannot provide, while keeping engineering and customer relationships in Singapore.
  • Data centre and AI infrastructure operators attracted by Johor's land and power, with Singapore as the commercial and connectivity hub.
  • Shared services and global services hubs: finance, HR, IT and customer operations serving a regional group.
  • Foreign companies entering ASEAN that want one Singapore HQ plus a cost-effective operating base, rather than choosing one country.
  • Singapore SMEs facing rising rents and labour costs that can move some work across the border without leaving Singapore.

Border and logistics improvements

  • Passport-free QR code clearance at Singapore's land checkpoints with Malaysia since March 2024.
  • A single transhipment permit with Singapore Customs, replacing two, for land intermodal transhipments from 1 January 2025.
  • RTS Link between Bukit Chagar (Johor Bahru) and Woodlands North: Singapore's LTA and Malaysia's Ministry of Transport said on 2 October 2026 that passenger service is now expected to start in February 2027, subject to safety certification and final approvals. Trial runs are due to finish by 31 December 2026.
  • Automated immigration lanes and paperless cargo clearance are planned for the future.

Setting it up: a step-by-step plan

  1. Decide what goes where. Map functions, people, assets and IP to the Singapore HQ or the Johor company before you incorporate anything.
  2. Incorporate the Singapore HQ (if you do not have one): usually within a day on BizFile. See how to register a company in Singapore.
  3. Pick the Johor site in the flagship zone that matches your activity, and get the IMFC-J location confirmation.
  4. Incorporate the Malaysian Sdn Bhd under the Singapore company, and register with SSM and the Malaysian tax authority.
  5. Apply to MIDA for the JS-SEZ incentive (or a standard MIDA incentive, not both). Advisers report around 3-6 months depending on the activity.
  6. Put intercompany agreements in place: manufacturing or service agreements, IP licences and transfer pricing documentation on both sides.
  7. Plan people: Employment Passes in Singapore (check with our COMPASS calculator) and Malaysian work permits or the knowledge worker rate in Johor.

Singapore companies can contact the JS-SEZ Joint Project Office run by Singapore's trade ministry, Enterprise Singapore and EDB for guidance, and may be able to fund overseas expansion costs through the EDGE grant.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the Singapore company gets the 5% rate. Only the Malaysian company in Johor can.
  • Choosing land first and checking incentives later. The flagship zone determines eligibility.
  • Claiming JS-SEZ and Pioneer Status together. They are mutually exclusive.
  • No transfer pricing documentation. Both tax authorities will look at the margin each side earns.
  • A Singapore HQ that is only a mailbox. It undermines Singapore tax residence, treaty access and bank relationships.
