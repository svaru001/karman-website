Korean Business Owners and Singapore: Holding Companies, Succession and the 2026-2027 Tax Changes

📅 Published 25 Nov 2026 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

Korea has one of the highest inheritance taxes in the world (a top rate of 50%, and up to 60% on a largest shareholder's shares), and it is widening its exit tax on people who emigrate, to include foreign shares from 1 January 2027. Singapore has no inheritance tax, no capital gains tax in most cases and no tax on dividends paid to shareholders. A Singapore company can be a strong regional headquarters, holding company or family office for Korean owners, but it does not remove Korean tax while the owner is still a Korean tax resident. The real benefit comes from genuine operations in Singapore, or from an owner who properly relocates.

  • Korea's top inheritance tax rate is 50%, rising to an effective 60% on largest-shareholder stakes.
  • Korea's exit tax on emigrating major shareholders (20% / 25%) extends to foreign shares from 1 Jan 2027.
  • Henley forecast a net outflow of 2,400 Korean millionaires in 2025, the fourth largest in the world.
  • Singapore: no inheritance tax, generally no capital gains tax, no tax on dividends to shareholders.
  • Korean residents are still taxed on worldwide income, and Korea's CFC rules can reach low-taxed subsidiaries.
  • Best fits: regional HQ for ASEAN expansion, family office, and owners who are genuinely relocating.

Korean founders and family business owners have been looking at Singapore for years, mostly for one reason: succession. A business built over a lifetime can lose more than half its value to tax when it passes to the next generation. In 2026 that pressure grew, with new proposals targeting large shareholders and a wider exit tax from 2027. This guide sets out what is changing in Korea, what Singapore offers, and, honestly, where a Singapore company helps and where it does not.

What is pushing Korean owners abroad

Inheritance and gift tax

Korea's top inheritance tax rate is 50%, the second highest in the OECD after Japan. When shares of a company's largest shareholder are valued for inheritance, a premium is added, which takes the effective maximum to about 60%. Proposals to remove that premium have been discussed but should not be relied on.

In 2026 the pressure moved in the other direction for listed companies. In August 2026, Korean media reported a government plan to raise inheritance and gift taxes on major shareholders of listed companies with persistently low valuations (price-to-book ratios at the bottom of their sector for years), with a valuation premium of at least 30%. The aim is to stop owners keeping share prices low ahead of succession. As of this writing it is a plan, not enacted law.

The exit tax is getting wider

Korea already taxes some emigrants on a deemed sale of their Korean shares when they leave. It applies to major shareholders of Korean companies who had a domicile or residence in Korea for more than five of the last ten years. Rates are 20% on the first KRW 300 million of deemed gains and 25% above that.

For departures on or after 1 January 2027, the exit tax extends to foreign shares, taxed at 20%, with no major shareholder test. There are exclusions, including where total foreign shares are worth KRW 500 million or less at departure. If you hold a Singapore company and plan to emigrate, the timing of your departure now matters.

The numbers

  • Henley & Partners forecast a net outflow of 2,400 millionaires from South Korea in 2025, double the 1,200 of 2024, ranking Korea fourth in the world after the UK, China and India.
  • Korean Ministry of Justice data analysed by the Korea Economic Daily showed 204 Koreans gave up citizenship to become Singaporean in 2023, up from 106 in 2022.

Where a Singapore company helps (and where it does not)

It helps when...

  • You are expanding into Southeast Asia. K-beauty, K-food, content, gaming, fintech and SaaS companies use Singapore as the regional headquarters for Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia. Profits earned by a Singapore company with real operations are taxed at Singapore rates. See setting up a regional HQ.
  • You are building a regional holding layer. A Singapore holding company can own ASEAN subsidiaries, receive dividends with Singapore tax exemptions where conditions are met, and sell subsidiaries without capital gains tax if it has substance.
  • The family is genuinely relocating. If the owner and heirs become Singapore tax residents and stop being Korean residents, future growth in assets held through Singapore can sit outside Korean inheritance tax. Korea still taxes Korean-situs assets, and the exit tax applies on departure.
  • You want a family office. A Singapore single family office using the 13O or 13U fund incentives can manage family wealth from Singapore. Note the new MAS notification requirement from 2027.

It does not help when...

  • You stay a Korean tax resident. Korean residents are taxed on worldwide income, and Korean inheritance tax applies to a Korean resident's worldwide assets. Putting shares in a Singapore company does not change that.
  • The Singapore company is passive. Korea's controlled foreign company rules can tax a Korean resident shareholder on undistributed profits of a low-taxed foreign company. A company with no staff or operations in Singapore is also unlikely to be treated as Singapore resident.
  • Management stays in Seoul. If key decisions are made in Korea, Korea may treat the company itself as Korean resident under its place of effective management rule.
  • You transfer the Korean company into Singapore. Moving shares of a Korean company to a Singapore holding company is itself a taxable disposal in Korea, and below-market transfers to family can trigger gift tax.

Practical steps for a Korean owner

  1. Map your Korean exposure first: residence status, major shareholder status, Korean-situs assets and the 2027 exit tax change. Get Korean advice before moving any shares.
  2. Decide the role of Singapore: operating HQ, holding company or family office. Each needs different substance.
  3. Incorporate: a Pte Ltd needs at least one locally resident director, a registered address and a company secretary. Registration usually completes in a day. See company formation for non-residents.
  4. Build substance: hire locally, hold board meetings in Singapore and give Singapore managers real authority.
  5. Move people: Employment Passes for Korean managers, or an EntrePass for founders. Check scores with our COMPASS calculator.
  6. Get a Certificate of Residence from IRAS once the company is managed from Singapore, to use the Korea-Singapore tax treaty.

Timing matters

If you are considering emigrating and already own foreign shares, including shares in a Singapore company, the extension of the exit tax to foreign shares from 1 January 2027 is worth discussing with your Korean adviser now. Inheritance and succession planning works best years ahead, not after a health event or a sale.
