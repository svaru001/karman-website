ACRA Changes from 20 October 2026: What Every Singapore Director Must Do

📅 Published 15 Nov 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

From 20 October 2026, Singapore companies filing an annual return must confirm that the details ACRA holds on their directors, CEO, secretary, auditors and members are accurate, and answer a new question on who performs the CEO role. The rule that a registered office must be open to the public for three hours a day is gone, and striking off now moves faster.

Key takeaways

  • Check every officer and shareholder record in Bizfile before you file your next annual return.
  • Decide who your "CEO" is - for most founder-run companies it is the founder who runs the business.
  • You can set your own registered office hours, but records must be available for inspection for at least two hours each business day on reasonable notice.
  • Gazette notices for striking off can now be published much sooner, so a struck-off company can disappear faster.

On 6 October 2026, ACRA announced that the next phase of the Corporate and Accounting Laws (Amendment) Act 2025 would take effect on 20 October 2026. Most of the changes are small on paper, but one of them changes what you are signing up to every time you file an annual return. If you are a director of a Singapore Pte Ltd - especially a foreign founder who relies on a corporate secretary - this is what you need to know.

1. Your annual return is now a confirmation of your records

When you (or your corporate secretary) file the annual return, you now also confirm that the particulars ACRA holds are accurate. According to ACRA, this covers the company's directors, CEO, secretaries, auditors and relevant members, and the particulars include:

  • Names, identification details and nationality
  • Contact addresses
  • Dates of appointment
  • The registered office address
  • Shareholding details, where applicable

Companies limited by guarantee must also confirm the total number of members last given to ACRA.

Why it matters: before, an out-of-date record was an oversight. Now, filing the annual return means positively confirming the record is right. Lodging false or misleading information with ACRA is an offence under the Companies Act, so "my secretary filed it" is a weaker defence than it used to be.

Common gaps we see

A director who moved overseas but never updated their contact address. A share transfer that was signed but never lodged. A nominee director whose ID document was renewed with a new number. A former CFO still listed as an officer. Fix each of these in Bizfile before you file the annual return.

2. The new CEO question

The annual return now asks whether any individual performs the role of chief executive officer. Under the Companies Act, a CEO is whoever is principally responsible for managing and running the company's business, whatever their job title. In a founder-run startup that is usually the founder, even if their title is "Director" or "Managing Director". If that person is not already recorded as CEO with ACRA, lodge the appointment before filing.

3. Registered office: no more 3-hour opening rule

The old rule required a registered office to be open and accessible to the public for at least three hours during normal business hours on every business day. That requirement has been removed - companies now set their own hours. Two duties remain:

  • On reasonable notice, company records kept at the registered office must be available for inspection for at least two hours on each business day.
  • Any change of registered office address must still be lodged with ACRA within 14 days.

For founders using a registered address service from their corporate secretary, nothing changes in practice. For founders using a home address, the rule is now easier to live with.

4. Striking off and restoration are faster

For a voluntary striking off application, ACRA can now publish the Gazette notice as early as the day it sends the notice to the company. For a striking off started by the Registrar, the Gazette notice can be published 15 days after ACRA's letter. Dissolution and restoration take effect at the date and time the Registrar acts, and are published in the Government Gazette.

ACRA must also refuse to restore a company that is likely to be used for an unlawful purpose or against public peace, welfare or good order, or where restoration would be against national security or interest.

What this means for you: if you are closing a company, the process can finish sooner. If you have a dormant company you mean to keep, make sure its filings are current - a company ACRA believes is not carrying on business can be struck off more quickly than before.

5. Public companies and accounting firms

Public companies with share capital no longer need to hold a statutory meeting or prepare a statutory report, and the "statement in lieu of prospectus" has been abolished. Public accountants can now renew their registration for one, two or three years, and professional indemnity insurance now applies to accounting firms that are sole proprietorships or partnerships. These changes do not affect a typical private limited company.

Your 15-minute checklist

  1. Log in to Bizfile and download your company's business profile.
  2. Check every director, secretary, auditor and shareholder: name, ID number, nationality, address, appointment date.
  3. Check share numbers against your register of members and any recent transfers or allotments.
  4. Decide who your CEO is and make sure ACRA's record matches.
  5. Lodge any corrections first, then file the annual return.
  6. Ask your corporate secretary to confirm they now run this check before every annual return.
