Singapore Company Registration Number (UEN): How to Search, Check and Verify a Company (2026)

📅 Published 24 Nov 2026 ⏱ 7 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

A Singapore company's registration number is its Unique Entity Number (UEN), issued by ACRA at incorporation. You can check any company's UEN, name, status (for example live or struck off) and registered address free on ACRA's BizFile search, with no login. For directors, shareholders and other details, buy a Business Profile from ACRA for S$5.50. Local companies have a 10-character UEN made of the incorporation year, five digits and a check letter (for example 202012889R); businesses such as sole proprietorships have nine characters.

Whether you are filling in a bank form, checking a new supplier or proving your own company exists, you will be asked for a Singapore company registration number. In Singapore that number is the UEN, or Unique Entity Number. This guide explains what a UEN is, how to read one, how to search any company for free, and when you need to buy an official ACRA document.

What is a UEN?

The UEN is the single identification number for every entity registered in Singapore: companies, sole proprietorships, partnerships, LLPs, societies and more. ACRA issues it to companies and businesses at registration, and every government agency uses it, including IRAS, CPF, MOM and Customs. When a form asks for your "company registration number", "ACRA registration number" or "business registration number", it means the UEN.

UEN formats

Entity typeFormatExample pattern
Local company (Pte Ltd, Ltd)10 characters: 4-digit year of incorporation + 5 digits + check letteryyyynnnnnX, e.g. 202012889R (incorporated in 2020)
Business (sole proprietorship, partnership)9 characters: 8 digits + check letternnnnnnnnX
Other entities (LLPs, foreign company branches, societies and others)10 characters: "T" or "S" + 2-digit year + 2-letter entity code + 4 digits + check letterTyyPQnnnnX, e.g. an LLP uses entity code "LL"

The year in a local company's UEN tells you when it was incorporated. A company claiming "20 years in business" with a 2024 UEN is worth a second question, though it may simply have restructured into a new entity.

How to search a Singapore company for free

  1. Go to BizFile, ACRA's portal, and use the free entity search. No Singpass or login is needed.
  2. Search by company name or UEN.
  3. The free result shows basic information, including the entity's name, UEN, status and registered address.

BizFile's free search also lets you check whether an entity is a registered corporate service provider (CSP). Do this before you hire anyone to incorporate your company or act as your nominee director.

You can also confirm a UEN on the government's UEN website, uen.gov.sg, which covers entities registered with ACRA and with other agencies.

What the entity status means

StatusWhat it means for you
LiveThe company exists and is registered. This says nothing about whether it is solvent or trading.
Struck offThe company has been removed from the register and legally no longer exists. Do not sign contracts with it.
In liquidation / winding upThe company is being closed. Payments and contracts need care.
DissolvedClosed after liquidation.

When to buy a Business Profile (S$5.50)

The free search does not show who owns or runs a company. For that, buy an ACRA Business Profile for S$5.50. It shows the UEN, entity name, registration date and position holders, typically including directors, shareholders, share capital, the company secretary and auditors. Banks, landlords, investors and foreign authorities usually ask for a recent Business Profile as proof of a company's details.

ACRA also sells other information products, including certificates, extracts and registers from S$11 and a People Profile at S$33, which lists the entities a person is connected to. All are bought on BizFile.

Is there a "certificate of incorporation" in Singapore?

When a company is incorporated, ACRA sends a notice of incorporation and a link to a free Business Profile, which must be downloaded within 60 days. For most purposes, such as opening a bank account, that Business Profile serves as proof of incorporation. If a foreign bank, embassy or counterparty wants a formal certificate, you can buy one from ACRA on BizFile. For use overseas it may also need to be legalised or apostilled. See our apostille guide.

Where to find your own company's UEN

  • the ACRA notification email you received at incorporation;
  • your Business Profile;
  • letters from IRAS, CPF or MOM;
  • your Corppass account.

Put it on your invoices, website footer and contracts. Customers and banks expect to see it.

UEN vs GST registration number

For GST-registered companies, the GST registration number is usually the same as the UEN, but not always. Check your IRAS GST registration letter, and always show the GST registration number on tax invoices once you are registered. See when to register for GST.

How to verify a Singapore company before you do business

  1. Match the name exactly to the invoice, contract and bank account name.
  2. Check the status is Live on the free BizFile search.
  3. Look at the UEN year and compare it with what the company says about its history.
  4. Buy a Business Profile (S$5.50) for significant deals to see who the directors and shareholders are.
  5. Check GST registration on IRAS if the supplier charges GST.
  6. Check the CSP register if the company claims to be a corporate service provider.

Changing details linked to your UEN

Your UEN never changes, even if you rename the company, move address or change directors. Those changes must be filed with ACRA, generally within 14 days, and your Business Profile updates automatically. Your company secretary normally handles these filings.
