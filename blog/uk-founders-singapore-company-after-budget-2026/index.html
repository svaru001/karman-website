UK Founders After the 2026 Budget: When a Singapore Company Makes Sense (and When It Does Not)

📅 Published 25 Nov 2026 ⏱ 10 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

UK tax on founders has risen in steps: employer NIC is 15%, dividend tax rose to 10.75% and 35.75% in April 2026, Business Asset Disposal Relief now taxes gains at 18%, and the non-dom regime was replaced by a residence-based system in April 2025. The 28 October 2026 Budget added a 10% Founders' Relief from April 2027 but announced a cut in the BADR lifetime limit. A Singapore company does not reduce UK tax for a founder who stays UK resident, because the company is likely to be UK resident too and dividends are taxed in the UK. It makes sense for founders building an Asian business with real operations in Singapore, or founders who genuinely relocate.

  • Dividend tax rates from 6 April 2026: 10.75%, 35.75% and 39.35%, with a £500 allowance.
  • BADR gains are taxed at 18% from 6 April 2026. Budget 2026 announced a new 10% Founders' Relief from April 2027.
  • Non-dom status ended in April 2025. Inheritance tax is now based on long-term UK residence.
  • A Singapore company run from the UK is usually UK tax resident. It is not a tax shortcut.
  • Real wins: an Asia headquarters with Singapore staff, or a genuine move to Singapore.
  • Returning to the UK within 5 years can bring gains and some dividends back into UK tax.

UK founders have had a lot of tax news in two years. Several high-profile founders have left, and "should I move my company to Singapore?" is a question we hear every week. The honest answer depends on one thing: where you, your team and your decisions will actually be. This guide sets out the UK changes, what Singapore offers, and the cases where a Singapore company genuinely helps.

The trap: a Singapore company run from the UK

Many founders assume that incorporating in Singapore means paying Singapore tax. For a UK-resident founder, it usually does not:

  • Company residence: under UK law a foreign company whose central management and control is in the UK is UK tax resident. If you run it from London, HMRC can tax its profits at 25%. The UK-Singapore tax treaty decides dual residence by place of effective management, which is again where decisions are made.
  • Dividends: a UK-resident shareholder pays UK dividend tax on dividends from a Singapore company at the same rates as UK dividends.
  • Controlled foreign companies: UK CFC rules can attribute profits artificially diverted to a low-taxed foreign subsidiary back to the UK parent.
  • Moving the UK company: transferring a UK company's business or IP to a new Singapore company is a disposal for UK tax, often at market value.

When a Singapore company genuinely makes sense

1. You are building in Asia

If your growth is in Southeast Asia, Australia, India or the Gulf, a Singapore company with its own team, customers and decision-makers earns profits that are properly taxed in Singapore. Fintech, B2B SaaS, professional services, e-commerce and AI companies commonly set up a Singapore regional headquarters under the UK parent. Foreign employees in Singapore do not attract employer social security, and Singapore offers grants and incentives for regional HQs.

2. You are genuinely relocating

Founders who move to Singapore, run the company from here and become non-resident in the UK under the statutory residence test can benefit from Singapore's rates on future income and growth. Three UK rules to plan around:

  • Temporary non-residence: if you return to the UK within five years, gains and some dividends from close companies received while abroad can be taxed when you come back.
  • Inheritance tax tail: long-term UK residents remain within UK IHT on worldwide assets for a period after leaving, of up to 10 years depending on how long they were resident.
  • Founders' Relief trade-off: leaving may cost you the new 10% rate if you sell after April 2027.

Employment Pass or EntrePass applications, a Singapore home and family moves all take time. See EntrePass for founders.

3. You are starting a new business abroad

Founders who plan to live outside the UK can start the new company in Singapore from day one, avoiding the cost and tax of migrating an existing UK company later. New Singapore companies can claim the start-up tax exemption on their first S$200,000 of profit for three years.

How UK founders set up in Singapore

  1. Incorporate a Pte Ltd on ACRA BizFile: usually within a day. You need at least one locally resident director, a registered office and a company secretary. Foreigners can own 100%.
  2. Open a bank account, with your UK company documents ready if the UK parent is the shareholder.
  3. Hire or relocate: Employment Passes for managers under COMPASS. Try our COMPASS calculator.
  4. Set transfer pricing for services, IP and recharges between the UK and Singapore companies.
  5. Get a Certificate of Residence once the company is managed in Singapore, to use the UK-Singapore tax treaty.
