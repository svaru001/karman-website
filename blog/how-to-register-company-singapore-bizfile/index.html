How to Register a Company in Singapore: The ACRA BizFile Process Step by Step (2026)

📅 Published 24 Nov 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

You register a Singapore company online through ACRA's BizFile portal in two stages: reserve a company name (S$15, usually reviewed within 3 working days and held for 120 days), then submit the incorporation application with the company's officers, shareholders, address, share capital and constitution, and pay S$300. Most applications are approved soon after payment, and you receive a Unique Entity Number (UEN) and a free Business Profile. Singapore Citizens, PRs and other Singpass holders can file themselves; foreigners without Singpass must file through an ACRA-registered corporate service provider.

  • Two stages on BizFile: name application (S$15), then incorporation (S$300).
  • The approved name is held for 120 days. You must incorporate within that window.
  • The person who reserves the name must file the registration and be appointed a director or secretary, unless a CSP files.
  • Most registrations are approved soon after payment; complex ones take up to 15 working days, and referrals to other agencies 14 to 60 days.
  • After approval: download the free Business Profile within 60 days, set up Corppass, open a bank account and appoint your secretary within 6 months.

Singapore is one of the fastest places in the world to set up a company. The whole registration runs online through ACRA's BizFile portal, and a straightforward application is often approved the same day it is paid for. The process still trips people up: names get rejected, the wrong person reserves the name, or founders discover halfway through that they don't have a qualifying local director. This guide walks through every step in order, based on ACRA's BizFile guidance, and flags the decisions you need to make before you log in.

Before you start: three things to settle

  1. Your structure. This guide is for a private limited company (Pte Ltd). If you are a freelancer or testing an idea, a sole proprietorship may be cheaper.
  2. Who files. Singapore Citizens, PRs and other individuals with Singpass can file on BizFile themselves. ACRA states that foreigners without Singpass must engage a corporate service provider (CSP), also called a registered filing agent, to reserve a name and register a company.
  3. Your local director. At least one director must be ordinarily resident in Singapore. Read the full requirements and documents checklist before starting.

Step 1: Choose and check your company name

Pick a name and two backups. ACRA rejects names that are identical to an existing company or a reserved name, or that are undesirable. Names using words like "bank", "insurance", "law", "school" or "university" are referred to other agencies for approval. Check before you pay:

  • search BizFile for existing entities with the same or very similar names;
  • run our Company Name Checker for ACRA's naming rules;
  • check that the domain and trademark are available. ACRA approval does not protect you from a trademark claim.

Step 2: Reserve the name on BizFile (S$15)

Log in to BizFile with Singpass, or have your CSP log in with its Corppass, and submit a name application. You will be asked for:

  • the proposed name and entity type (local company, private limited);
  • the primary (and optional secondary) business activity as an SSIC code. Find yours with the SSIC Code Search;
  • payment of the S$15 fee, which is not refunded if the name is rejected or withdrawn.

Important: ACRA requires that the person who reserves the name is the one who files the incorporation, and that they are appointed a director or secretary of the new company. The exception is when a CSP files. Don't let a friend or assistant reserve the name for you.

ACRA aims to review names within 3 working days; referrals to other agencies can take up to 15 working days. Once approved, the name is reserved for 120 days.

Step 3: Prepare the incorporation details

Gather everything before you open the form, as BizFile sessions time out. You need:

SectionWhat to enter
Company detailsApproved name, financial year end, registered office address, company email
Share capitalNumber of shares, currency, issued and paid-up amounts (minimum S$1), share class
ShareholdersName, ID or registration number, nationality, address and number of shares for each
OfficersDirectors (at least one locally resident) and, if appointed now, the company secretary: NRIC/FIN or passport, date of birth, residential and contact addresses, mobile, email
ConstitutionUpload your own or adopt ACRA's model constitution
RegistersDeclarations on the Register of Registrable Controllers and the Register of Nominee Directors and Shareholders

Choosing a financial year end (FYE). Your FYE drives every future deadline: ECI within 3 months, the annual return within 7 months and the tax return in the following year. Many founders pick 31 December for simplicity. Others pick a date 12 months from incorporation to make the first period as long as possible. Model both in the Compliance Deadline Calculator.

Step 4: Submit the incorporation and pay S$300

Within the 120-day reservation window, open the local company registration form on BizFile, enter the details above, review, and pay the S$300 incorporation fee. If a CSP files for you, it collects each director's signed consent to act and handles the submission.

Step 5: Approval and your UEN

ACRA says most registrations are approved soon after payment. Complex applications can take up to 15 working days, and applications referred to other agencies 14 to 60 days. On approval you receive:

  • a notice of successful incorporation;
  • your UEN (Unique Entity Number), the company's ID for every government agency. See how to look up and use a UEN;
  • a link to a free Business Profile. Download it within 60 days or the link expires. Banks will ask for it.

If your application is rejected or queried, our guide to why ACRA rejects incorporations covers the usual fixes.

Step 6: The first 30 days after incorporation

  1. Set up Corppass (possible one day after the UEN is issued). You need it for IRAS, CPF, MOM and grant portals.
  2. Open a corporate bank account. Bring the Business Profile, constitution and a board resolution. Compare options with our bank account recommender.
  3. Issue share certificates and set up the statutory registers: members, directors, secretaries, auditors, controllers and nominees.
  4. Pay in the share capital you declared.
  5. Apply for work passes if you or your team will work in Singapore. Check eligibility with the EP COMPASS Calculator.

How long does it take overall?

For a local founder with a clean name and standard activity, name approval plus incorporation can be done in 1 to 3 working days. Foreign founders should allow extra time for KYC with the filing agent and for arranging a resident director. Opening a bank account usually takes longer than incorporation itself.
