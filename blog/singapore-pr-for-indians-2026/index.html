How to Get Singapore PR as an Indian Professional or Founder (2026): Eligibility, Process and Status Check

📅 Published 17 Nov 2026 ⏱ 9 min read ✍️ Karman Editorial Team

Quick answer

Indians who hold an Employment Pass or S Pass can apply for Singapore Permanent Residence (PR) online through ICA without a sponsor, as can spouses and young children of Singapore Citizens or PRs, and foreign investors. The fee is S$100 per applicant, ICA generally decides within 6 months of a complete application, and status is checked online or via the MyICA portal. ICA does not publish a points system; it assesses each application as a whole.

Key takeaways

  • EP and S Pass holders apply without a sponsor; spouses and children of Singapore Citizens or PRs need the family member to sponsor.
  • Fee: S$100 per applicant on submission, non-refundable. Completion fees after approval: S$20 entry permit, S$50 re-entry permit, S$50 IC.
  • ICA generally processes complete applications within 6 months. Check status online or on MyICA.
  • You keep your Indian passport: PR is residence status, not citizenship.
  • Sons who become PRs are generally liable for National Service. Factor this in before including children.

If you are an Indian professional on an Employment Pass, or a founder running a Singapore company, this is how Permanent Residence actually works in 2026, based on ICA's own guidance.

Who can apply for Singapore PR

ICA accepts PR applications in these categories:

CategorySponsor needed?
Employment Pass or S Pass holderNo
Spouse of a Singapore Citizen or PRYes, the spouse
Unmarried child under 21 of a Singapore Citizen or PRYes, the parent
Aged parent of a Singapore CitizenYes, the citizen child (21+)
Student in Singapore who has passed a national exam (PSLE, N, O or A Level) or is in the Integrated ProgrammeNo
Foreign investor, including the Global Investor Programme (GIP) run by EDBNo

For most Indians reading this, the route is the first one: work in Singapore on an EP or S Pass, build a track record, and apply. Your spouse and unmarried children under 21 can be included in the same application.

What ICA looks at

There is no published points system and no minimum salary or years-of-stay rule. ICA assesses each application as a whole. Factors applicants and advisers commonly point to include:

  • Economic contribution: salary level, stability of employment and taxes paid in Singapore.
  • Length of stay and continuity on a work pass.
  • Qualifications and age.
  • Family ties to Singapore, such as a Singaporean spouse or children studying locally.
  • Integration: community involvement, local social ties and a clear commitment to settling down.

Because the assessment is holistic, an application with a stable EP history, a salary comfortably above the COMPASS benchmarks and a family that already lives in Singapore is generally in a stronger position than one filed a few months after arrival.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Collect documents using ICA's document checklist: passports, birth and marriage certificates, educational certificates, employment and salary records, and recent payslips. Non-English documents need official translations.
  2. Apply online through ICA's PR e-Service using Singpass.
  3. Pay S$100 per applicant (card, internet direct debit or PayNow). It is non-refundable.
  4. Wait for the outcome. ICA generally processes complete applications within 6 months; some take longer, and incomplete applications can be rejected or delayed by requests for more documents.

How to check your Singapore PR application status

ICA emails the outcome when a decision is made. In the meantime you can check the status online through ICA's e-Service or the MyICA portal using Singpass. There are no detailed progress updates while the application is being assessed, so a status that stays unchanged for months is normal. Do not file a second application while the first is pending.

If you are approved

ICA issues an In-Principle Approval letter. You then complete formalities in person at the ICA Services Centre (with your sponsor, if you had one) and pay the completion fees:

ItemFee
Entry PermitS$20
Re-Entry Permit (5 years)S$50
Singapore Identity Card (blue IC, age 15+)S$50

Keep a valid Re-Entry Permit. Without one, you cannot travel overseas and return as a PR.

What changes for Indians after PR

  • You keep your Indian passport. PR is a residence status. India does not allow dual citizenship, but PR is not citizenship, so your Indian passport is unaffected.
  • CPF: you and your employer start contributing to CPF, at graduated rates in the first two years.
  • No more work pass: you no longer depend on your employer's EP, and you can switch jobs or start a company without a new pass.
  • National Service: male PRs and sons who become PRs are generally liable for National Service. This is the single biggest decision point for families with boys.
  • Property: PRs can buy resale HDB flats (with conditions) and pay lower stamp duty on private property than foreigners.
  • Indian tax and FEMA: your Indian residential status for tax and FEMA depends on days spent in India, not on Singapore PR. Read our guide to becoming an NRI in Singapore.

For founders: PR through your own company

If you run your own Singapore company, you can hold an Employment Pass as its director and later apply for PR as an EP holder. ICA will look at the business behind you, so a company with real revenue, local hires, a clean compliance record and a salary that keeps pace with the EP floor (S$6,000 for new applications from 1 January 2027) is a stronger foundation than a shell. Read how founders get an EP for their own company and EntrePass vs Employment Pass.

Founders with significant capital can also look at EDB's Global Investor Programme, which grants PR directly to qualifying investors and business owners. Its criteria and investment options are set by EDB and are well beyond most early-stage founders.

Common reasons PR applications fail

  • Applying too soon after arriving, with little Singapore employment history.
  • Frequent job changes or gaps between passes.
  • Missing or untranslated documents.
  • A salary near the bottom of the work pass range.
  • Little evidence of ties to Singapore beyond work.

ICA does not give reasons for rejection. Many applicants reapply after strengthening their profile, typically after at least several months.
